# 📖 Steve's Journal

## ✦ Chapter 4 — The Warehouse

> *"I know I have it somewhere."*
>
> - Steve

---

## ✦ The Problem

I have begun to notice a rather embarrassing problem.  I have too much stuff.  This is not a new problem.  Over the years I have collected nearly everything imaginable.  Stone.  Wood.  Iron.  Food.  Tools.  Weapons.  Materials from places I probably should not have visited.  Rare items that I refuse to throw away because I am certain they will eventually become useful.  The problem is not collecting these things.  The problem is finding them again.  I have chests everywhere.  In the workshop.  In the house.  In the barns.  In the mine.  Near the farms.  Even in places I had completely forgotten about until I stumbled across them again.  Every time I need something, I find myself opening chest after chest.  Sometimes I know exactly where an item should be.  Sometimes I am certain I have it.  Sometimes I spend ten minutes searching before finally discovering that I used the last of it three weeks ago.  There has to be a better way.

---

## ✦ The First Attempt

My first solution was simple.  More chests.  Naturally, this did not solve anything.  More chests simply created more places for things to hide.  So I tried organizing them.  One chest for stone.  One for wood.  One for ores.  One for food.  One for tools.  That worked for a while.  Then I found myself with three chests for stone.  Two for wood.  Four for miscellaneous materials.  And one chest that I had apparently decided should contain anything that didn't belong anywhere else.  I have no idea what is in that chest.  I am almost afraid to look.  The problem was becoming obvious.  I didn't need more storage.  I needed a better way to **manage** what I already had.

---

## ✦ The Idea

I began thinking about the magic I had discovered in my earlier experiments.  The Disenchanter had taught me that magic could be separated from an object and preserved.  The MobCages had taught me that something could be contained without needing to remain physically where it was placed.  Perhaps I could do something similar with items.  What if the storage itself did not actually need to contain the items?  What if the items could be recorded somewhere else?  I began experimenting with magical records.  At first I tried storing the physical items themselves.

That was a disaster.

The chest became heavier than a mountain.  I am still not entirely certain how that is possible.  So I tried something different.

Instead of storing the items...

I would store **what the items were**.  Their identity.  Their quantity.  Their properties.  And then I would let the magic keep track of everything else.

---

## ✦ The Discovery

The first successful test was almost anticlimactic.  I placed a collection of stone into the system.  The physical items disappeared from the chest.  For a moment, I thought I had destroyed them.  Then I checked the record.  There they were.  Every piece.  Every count.  Still accounted for.  I added more.  The number increased.  I removed some.  The number decreased.  I added them again.  The system remembered.  I stood there staring at the results.
 For the first time, I wasn't looking at a pile of items.  I was looking at an **inventory**.  A single place where I could see everything I had stored without having to search through dozens of chests.

I had finally found the solution.

---

## ✦ The Warehouse

I decided to call it a **Warehouse**.  Not because it was a building.  In fact, it didn't need to be.  The Warehouse was the collection itself.  Every item could have a place within it.  Materials could be organized.  Items could be grouped.  Things that didn't yet have a proper home could still be stored rather than forgotten.

And most importantly...

I could finally stop building walls out of chests.

---

## ✦ The Categories

There was still one problem.  Once I had everything in one place, I had created another enormous pile of things.  A magical pile...

But still a pile.

So I began organizing. I created categories.  Stone.  Wood.  Ores.  Food.  Tools.  And then I realized some things needed more organization than that.  Certain items belonged together, but also needed their own sections.  So I created smaller sections within the larger ones.  The system began to take shape.  It was no longer simply a place to put things.

It was a way to **understand what I had**.

---

## ✦ The Forgotten Items

There were items in my storage that I had completely forgotten about.  Things I had collected months ago.  Rare materials I thought I had lost.  Items I had searched for repeatedly, only to eventually give up.  They had been there the entire time.  Buried beneath everything else.  That may be the most useful part of the Warehouse. It doesn't just store what I have.  It helps me remember what I have.

---

## ✦ A Better Way

I think I finally understand something.  My earlier inventions solved individual problems.  The MobStand gave me a way to make work happen without me.  The Disenchanter gave me a way to preserve magic that would otherwise be lost.  The MobCages gave me a way to contain and move creatures.  The Warehouse is different.  It doesn't make something easier to do.  It makes it easier to **know**.  I no longer need to remember which chest contains the iron.
 I don't need to remember which building contains the wood.  I don't need to wonder whether I have something buried in a forgotten corner of my world.  The Warehouse remembers for me.

And after everything else I have built...

I think that may be one of the most useful discoveries yet.

---

## ✦ There Is Still More

I have only just begun experimenting with the Warehouse.  There are still questions I need to answer.  Can I make it easier to find a specific item?  Can I track where something came from?  Can items be moved between categories without losing their identity?  Can different locations access the same collection?  Can I determine exactly how much of something I have without searching for it?

I suspect the answer to all of these questions is yes.

Which means, naturally...

I have more work to do.

---

## ✦ The Warehouse

What started as an attempt to solve my own storage problems has become something much larger.  A place where everything can have a home.  A place where nothing needs to be forgotten.  A place where I can finally answer the question I have asked myself more times than I care to admit:

**"Where did I put that?"**

Perhaps I won't have to ask it anymore.

---

## ✦ A Note About My Work

The Warehouse began as a simple experiment to solve a problem I had created for myself.  Too many items.  Too many chests.  Too many places to look.  I have refined the system so that others can put it to use as well.  If your own world has become buried beneath mountains of storage, perhaps it is time to stop searching through chests and let the Warehouse keep track of it for you.
